A. BLOCKERS: fix before optimising
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A1. Background figure of merit is saturated
    STATUS: OPEN
    The pair background is 2-3 surviving pairs out of 196,736 candidates (-> ~1e-5 events in
    15 years). Every reasonable design gives ~0, so differences between designs are Poisson
    noise.
    Plan: score designs on the single-muon veto inefficiency per muon type (large statistics),
    build the pair background by factorisation, rate_pairs x eps_1 x eps_2 (check that this holds
    on the pairs we do have), and quote an upper limit when there are zero survivors.

A2. Bounced muons: the veto barely sees them
    STATUS: OPEN
    Only 74-77 % of bounced SST1 muons are vetoed. The search window contains the true crossing
    for only 30 % of them: a straight line extrapolated back from the spectrometer cannot follow
    the bounce off the cavern wall / floor. This term will probably decide the fin layout.
    Plan: study this class on its own (where it crosses the planes, before/after the bounce
    point), consider planes downstream of the bounce point or a search aimed at the wall.
    Statistics: rock_bounced and veto_only come from ONE simulation run (overfitting risk).
    The energy loss in the cavern rock is well modelled in the stored muons (see C4).

A3. Rate limits are reported, not enforced
    STATUS: DONE (4 Nov 2026: steep penalty in the objective + fine peak maps near the flames)
    The 4 cm tiles on the z = 30.6 m fins reach 10 MHz per channel (limit 1 MHz; consistent with
    SR-370's "up to 10 MHz in the flames", a good sanity check). The straw fin at z = 30.6 m is
    above 0.5 MHz/cm^2. A design over its limit still gets a normal score.
    Plan: rate limits as hard constraints or penalties. The fin rate maps have 20 x 20 cm bins,
    which averages the peak down for smaller cells: use finer maps near the flames.
    4 Nov 2026, done (Guglielmo: steep penalty in the objective):
      - objective.rate_penalty: every limit exceeded costs w_rate * (1 + rate_slope * (peak / limit - 1)),
        w_rate = 1000 (> all other terms together), rate_slope = 10 (ObjectiveConfig). Was 1 + log10(peak / limit)
        since 15 Oct (so a penalty existed, but grew only logarithmically above the limit). 2 x over = 11 000 points.
      - fine peak maps: bin/make_peak_maps.py -> results/rate_maps/peak_maps.npz, 2 x 2 cm^2 maps (sum w, sum w^2)
        from all 506 M muons of Full_dataset_z_3060_8200.pkl: UBT x-y at 30.6 m and fins at z = 30.6 - 40.6 m,
        u < 2 m. reco.plane_rates takes the peak over a sliding window of the channel size (pads) or 4 cm (strips),
        only windows with >= 10 effective muons, and keeps max(fine, coarse). Same normalisation as the coarse maps
        (totals and 20 cm peaks agree to 0.1 %).
      - effect at z = 30.6 m (bin/check_peak_rates.py, results/rate_maps/check_peak_rates.json): peak density
        634 -> 880 +- 5 kHz/cm^2 (right fin), 459 -> 642 (left), x 1.39; 4 cm tile 10.1 -> 14.1 MHz per channel,
        2 cm tile 2.5 -> 3.6 MHz (still over 1 MHz). Penalties: D6_straw 1 103 -> 12 435, D6_tile2 2 668 -> 43 554,
        D6_tile 7 578 -> 253 426; E3_robust / E4 designs stay feasible (0).
      - remaining (not A3): fibre and pixel have no rate limit in technology.py, so are never penalised; fins
        beyond z = 40.6 m and u > 2 m still use the coarse peak (rates there are >= 3 x lower). The evolution runs on
        the cluster: copy results/rate_maps/peak_maps.npz into its results/ (without it plane_rates falls back to
        the coarse maps, as before).

A5. Spill and PoT normalisation
    STATUS: OPEN
    Accidental hits use flat times inside a 1 s spill (--spill). config.py has 5e13 PoT per spill
    and 6e20 PoT in 15 years. Check that rates match the 5e13 PoT/s convention, and model the real
    spill structure (50 Hz ripple, duty factor): accidentals scale with the instantaneous rate,
    and the straw signal loss (5.3 % with a +-25 ns window) scales with them.


A6. Spectrometer field map not used: products propagated in straight lines
    STATUS: OPEN (added 4 Nov 2026)
    Downstream of SST1 (z = 82 m) every track (muons, DIS products, V0 daughters, signal daughters) is
    extrapolated in a STRAIGHT line to the timing detector (z = 92.71 m, muon_types.py:91, reco.py:383),
    through the spectrometer magnet (z = 84.36-89.56 m, |x| < 2.7, |y| < 3.8 m, geometry.SPECTROMETER_Z).
    The field map MainSpectrometerField.h5 (z = 77.06-96.86 m, io.load_field_map) is only used for drawing
    (bin/visualize_detector.py). So the timing-detector acceptance, the "single track" / <= 2 tracks in
    the acceptance cuts and every rate after the timing-detector cut ignore the bending: soft tracks
    (1 - few GeV) that the field sweeps out of (or into) the 2 x 3 m timing plane are counted wrongly, and
    opposite-charge pairs separate in x. The p > 1 GeV cut uses the true momentum, not a measurement.
    Plan:
      - propagate EVERY charged product from its state at z = 82 m to the timing plane through the field map
        (RK4 in B from MainSpectrometerField.h5, charge from the pdg code), vectorised (numpy / torch) since
        it runs on all candidates; neutral particles stay straight;
      - use the propagated position for the timing-detector acceptance and for the track-multiplicity cuts;
      - optionally measure p from the bending (SST1/2 before, SST3/4 after the magnet) with a resolution model,
        and apply the p > 1 GeV cut on the measured p;
      - redo the SST1 / TD / p > 1 GeV rates (now 30.6 / 15.3 / 8.5 kHz) and compare with FairShip
        (48.5 / 14.6 / 7.1 kHz, which has the field).

A7. Detector planes have no predefined module dimensions
    STATUS: OPEN (added 4 Nov 2026, module sizes from Guglielmo)
    Plane sizes are free and continuous (fin "length", "y_lo" / "y_hi", UBT extent in design.py); channels and
    cost are computed for exactly that area (technology.n_channels rounds cells, not modules). Only the straw
    length (max_length 1 m) and the fibre mat length (3 m) are quantised. A real plane is assembled from
    fixed modules, so the optimiser can propose sizes that cannot be built, and module borders are ignored.
    Modules to use (one module carries all the layers of the technology):
        straw   1 x 1 m^2, all 6 layers (3 views 0, +-15 deg, 2 staggered layers each), 5 mm pitch
        tile2   50 x 50 cm^2 of 2 x 2 cm tiles (25 x 25 = 625 SiPMs)
        tile    48 x 48 cm^2 of 4 x 4 cm tiles (12 x 12 = 144 SiPMs)
        fiber   0.5 x 2.5 m^2 mat (SCSF-78MJ, 250 um), two crossed mats (x, y) per module
                (max_length 3 m -> 2.5 m)
        pixel   quad ~4 x 4 cm^2 (2 x 2 Timepix4-like chips)
    Modules of EVERY technology are assembled the same way: side by side (an n_u x n_y grid in the plane, e.g.
    4 straw modules = 2 x 2 m) and stacked in z at the same (x, y) (redundant stations, cf. E4_ubt2 / E4_entry2).
    Technologies can be MIXED in one plane: e.g. 1 m in x = two 50 x 50 cm tile2 modules, or one tile2 module
    + one 0.5 m wide fibre mat, and so on (fine timing / granularity only where the rate needs it).
    Note: module sizes are not all on the same grid (tile 48 cm vs 50 cm, fibre 2.5 m vs straw 1 m in y):
    decide the common grid (e.g. 50 cm, tile modules padded to 50 cm) or allow mismatched borders as dead area.
    Each module has a DEAD FRAME (frame, SiPMs / readout, manifolds): the border is inefficient (links C2).
    Plan:
      - technology.py: module size (u, y), cells per module, dead-frame width per technology (widths to be fixed
        with the detector colleagues), channels and cost PER MODULE;
      - design.py: a plane = a grid of module slots, each slot = (technology, number of stacked layers in z);
        the optimiser's free parameters become these integers / choices (continuous length / y range removed);
      - reco.py: zero efficiency in the dead frames (and in the gaps between modules), material of the frames
        in the MS / DIS budget;
      - rate limits (A3) per module channel; redo the existing designs (D6_*, E3/E4) on the module grid.

B1. Multiple scattering (at least this interaction must be modelled)
    STATUS: OPEN (material budget defined 15 Oct 2026, ready to implement)
    The stored muons have NO multiple scattering after the shield: the states at z = 30.6 m and
    z = 82 m are joined by straight lines and the transport did not include the air / vessel.
    Material along a muon's path (now fully defined, so MS can be computed for every muon):
        z = 29.89 - 30.6 m   air, 0.71 m        (shield end, TRY6, to the UBT)    x/X0 = 0.0023
        z = 30.6 m           Mylar wall, 350 um (decay-vessel entrance)           x/X0 = 0.0012
        z = 30.6 - 80.6 m    helium, 50 m       (decay volume, SR-370 balloon)    x/X0 = 0.0088
        z = 80.6 m           Mylar wall, 350 um (decay-vessel exit)               x/X0 = 0.0012
        z = 80.6 - 82 m      air, 1.4 m         (to SST1)                         x/X0 = 0.0046
        + every veto plane crossed (technology.x0: straw 0.12 %, fibre 0.6 %, pixel 0.5 %,
          tile 2.4 % X0 per plane)
        X0 used: air 303.9 m, helium 5671 m, Mylar 28.54 cm (PDG; walls were 0.3 mm PVC, X0 19.63 cm, until 2026-11-04).
    Total for a straight muon through the vessel: x/X0 = 0.0182 without the UBT, 0.019 with a
    straw UBT, 0.043 with a tile UBT. Highland, theta0 = 13.6 MeV / p * sqrt(x/X0) *
    (1 + 0.038 ln(x/X0)): 1.6 mrad / p[GeV] (straw UBT), 2.5 mrad / p[GeV] (tile UBT).
    Displacement at SST1 (z = 82 m) from one scatterer at p = 1 GeV: PVC entrance ~2 cm,
    straw UBT ~2 cm, tile UBT ~9 cm, PVC exit ~0.1 cm. Small at high momentum, relevant for the
    soft muons (1 - few GeV) that dominate the fin rates.
    Bounced muons: apply MS only AFTER the bounce point (muon_types.bounce_points), i.e. from the
    bounce on the cavern wall / floor to z = 82 m in air plus whatever planes / walls it crosses
    after it. The path before the bounce and the energy loss in the rock are already in the
    transport (C4).
    Plan:
      - for every muon, walk its true path (A -> K -> B) through the list above, sum x/X0 per
        segment, apply Gaussian angle and position kicks per segment (variance proportional to
        x_i/X0, scaled so the total matches Highland for the full x/X0), momentum from the stored
        states;
      - use the scattered path for the own hits on the UBT and the fins, and the scattered state at
        SST1 for the spectrometer measurement;
      - open: material of the vessel SIDE walls (muons entering through the SBT walls) is not
        defined yet;
      - check that reco.RecoConfig.ms_slope / r_ms (search window growth with 1/p) still cover
        the true scatter, otherwise the window loses veto efficiency or grows the accidentals.

B2. EM debris rate (electrons / photons from the end of the shield)
    STATUS: OPEN
    Only muons are simulated: no delta rays, bremsstrahlung or pair-production debris leaking from
    the shield. These add hits (occupancy, rate limits) and accidental vetoes (signal loss).
    Plan (estimate from data we have):
      - take the full dataset (data/Full_dataset_z_3060_8200.pkl, 506 M rows of ONE run, seed 42):
        every muon that reaches z = 30.6 m, with its momentum and position;
      - for each, compute the probability that a muon of that energy produces an electron or
        photon in the last 20 cm of iron of the last shield magnet (z = 29.69-29.89 m) that
        escapes it: delta rays (dN/dT ~ 0.56 MeV/cm / T^2 in iron, for T << T_max), bremsstrahlung
        and direct pair production, with the escape length set by the electron range / photon
        conversion length (X0 iron = 1.76 cm, so 20 cm = 11.4 X0 is effectively the equilibrium
        layer);
      - turn that into e / gamma rate maps on the UBT and the fins (same binning as the muon maps),
        apply a per-technology detection probability (photons convert rarely in thin straws, more
        in 1 cm tiles) and add them to the accidental hits.
    Cross-check later against a Geant4 run of a muon beam on 20 cm of iron.

B3. Neutrino and muon deep inelastic scattering
    STATUS: DEFERRED (to be evaluated later)
    Not taken into account: DIS in the veto planes (tiles 2.4 % X0 vs straws 0.12 % X0), in the
    vessel walls and in the cavern. Until then the optimiser will favour thick fast planes for
    free; keep the material reported (x0 per plane) so it can be added as a constraint.

B4. Signal model
    STATUS: OPEN
    Now: neutral particle decaying uniformly in the vessel volume, two daughters uniform over the
    SST1 acceptance, p uniform 1-50 GeV. Signal is lost only through accidental hits.
    Plan: use an HNL (and dark photon) model from the literature:
      - decay position: probability to decay in the vessel P = exp(-L1/lambda) - exp(-L2/lambda),
        lambda = beta*gamma*c*tau; for the long lifetimes near the sensitivity limit
        (lambda >> 50 m) the vertex is close to uniform in z, otherwise exponential;
      - momentum spectrum and production from charm / beauty meson decays at 400 GeV, and
        decay channels (e.g. HNL -> mu pi, e pi, mu mu nu): take them from the FairShip HNL
        generator or from the references below, NOT invented;
      - daughter opening angles from the 2-body / 3-body kinematics, so the daughters' spread
        in the SST1 acceptance is realistic.
    References:
      [1] SHiP Collaboration, "Sensitivity of the SHiP experiment to Heavy Neutral Leptons",
          JHEP 04 (2019) 077, arXiv:1811.00930.
      [2] K. Bondarenko, A. Boyarsky, D. Gorbunov, O. Ruchayskiy, "Phenomenology of GeV-scale
          Heavy Neutral Leptons", JHEP 11 (2018) 032, arXiv:1805.08567  (production and decay
          widths, branching ratios).
      [3] SHiP Collaboration, "Sensitivity of the SHiP experiment to dark photons decaying to
          a pair of charged particles", Eur. Phys. J. C 81 (2021) 451, arXiv:2011.05115.
      [4] S. Alekhin et al., "A facility to Search for Hidden Particles at the CERN SPS: the SHiP
          physics case", Rep. Prog. Phys. 79 (2016) 124201, arXiv:1504.04855.
      [5] SPSC-P-369 (CERN-SPSC-2023-033), BDF/SHiP ECN3 proposal: signal selection, IP and veto
          requirements (summarised in conversation.txt).
      (Check volume / page numbers against INSPIRE before citing in a talk.)

D3. MS7 shield DIS -> K_L: chain disagrees with FairShip
    STATUS: OPEN (PRIORITY B)
    Forced chain in the last 50 cm of MS7: K_L decays in the vessel ~4.9 kHz, (6.73 +- 0.59)e7 per 15 y
    after DOCA < 1 cm, fiducial, IP < 2.5 m (docs/shield_iron_dis.md).
      - plain Geant4 (bin/shield_natural_check.py) gives 0.52 +- 0.15 of the chain's K_L; the excess is the
        Pythia 6 component of mu_library_iron (9.5 % of interactions, 90 % of the K_L);
      - FairShip full physics (TRY_2026_EM_SST1_origin) sees ZERO K_L / K_S from the shield reaching the SST,
        and shield-made pi ~17x below the chain (not explained by the Pythia excess);
      - after the full selection the mu number rests on 3-7 MC events (+-40-60 %);
      - algorithm A cannot veto it (decay tracks have no upstream hits); asymmetry cuts at 0.9 keep 100 %.
    Plan: find the FairShip discrepancy (MS7 flux / field TRY6 vs TRY2026, Kokoulin muon-nuclear in FairShip
    vs Pythia 6 high-nu DIS here; the flux in the MS7 footprint already agrees); K_L-enriched draws; model
    a veto on any in-time hit (scattered muon in UBT / fins) and a pointing cut.
