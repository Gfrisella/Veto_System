## 3. Changes to existing code

- **`bin/make_design_explorer.py`: bug fix in the UBT rate map.** Type 7, the muons stopping in the shield, were
  counted on the UBT (5.3e9 instead of 1.05e7 muons/spill), which made every design infeasible on delta.
  The map now excludes type 7 and requires `z_30 > Z_UBT - 0.05`. Alpha and gamma were unaffected.
- **`bin/benchmark_designs.py`: `candidates_blocked()`.** Pairs are built per spill in blocks, so the 25 M delta
  candidates fit in memory. It gives the same result as `candidates()` on alpha (196,736 pairs, same weight).
- **`bin/subsample_candidates.py`: Poisson sampling proportional to weight.**
  - 25.2 M -> 400 k candidates; 48 k are kept with certainty.
  - Unbiased for every weighted sum; sampling error 0.05 %.
  - Used for everything below: `results/reco/inputs_delta_s.npz`.
- **`bin/replace_rate_maps.py`:** patches corrected rate maps into an existing reco-inputs file.
- **`lib/veto/vertex_selection.py`:** one selection for every sample: measured IP < 0.1 m and at most 2 tracks in
  the SST1 acceptance.
- **`lib/veto/interactions.py`:** fast-veto-sim's forced interactions (muon DIS, GENIE neutrinos) through the same
  selection and veto.
  - Per target: helium, air, PVC walls, every plane.
  - The scattered muon's path starts from its parent's 30.6 m state and kinks at the vertex, so the parent's real
    upstream hits are digitised.
  - Veto levels: (a) the two vertex tracks; (b) every reconstructed track; (c) a truth bound.
- **`lib/veto/reco.py`: vertex cone (`RecoConfig.cone`, per design `"detector": {"cone_mrad": ...}`).**
  - Upstream of the vertex the search radius is `max(r_A, r0 + cone * (z_vertex - z_plane))`. This recovers the
    parent of a muon interaction whose scattered muon kinked by a few mrad at the vertex.
  - It costs signal through random hits in the larger window.
- **`lib/veto/scattering.py`:** ParticleShaker-style multiple scattering with tails.
- **`lib/veto/objective.py`: interactions as sources.**
  - `score()` adds every interaction source in `res["interactions"]` to N, so N = combinatorial + mu + nu.
  - Otherwise the objective is unchanged; see section 6 for the problem this exposes.
- **Interaction drivers** (`bin/prepare_interactions.py`, `bin/run_interactions.py`, `bin/scan_cone.py`):
  - `bin/prepare_interactions.py` caches the selected interactions independently of the design:
    `results/interactions/prepared_{muon,nu}_delta.npz`.
  - `bin/run_interactions.py` runs one design.
  - `bin/scan_cone.py` scans the cone.
- **`evolve/`: backend and inputs options.**
  - `--backend codex` (Codex CLI, model from `~/.codex/config.toml`) alongside claude and openrouter.
  - `--inputs` and `--prep-tag` to evolve on any dataset; interactions are included in the objective.

| D6_tile2 | 10 / 20 | 5.9 / 17.8 % | 1.0 | 0.4 | 1.5 / 1.4 | 4.4 | over rate limit (30.6 m tile fins) |

What the table shows:
- **Already zero after the veto:** combinatorial falls from 16.8 to ~1e-5. Muon interactions in the PVC walls
  (128) and in the planes (80) are all vetoed. Air and PVC for both muons and neutrinos are 0 after selection.
- **What survived in E3, and why:** E3's surviving muon interactions are all parents that crossed one plane (the
  UBT or a 30.6 m fin) and left no hit; per-plane efficiency is 0.999. A second station at the entrance fixes this,
  and the cone fixes the kink.
- **What is left:** about 0.6 neutrino DIS in the helium. The vertex is in the gas, nothing charged comes in, and
  the third that is vetoed is vetoed by random hits, the same way signal is lost.
- **Not modelled yet:** tagging the neutrino event's own extra products (wide-angle hadrons hitting fins downstream
  of the vertex, SBT-style). `interactions.charged_tracks` keeps only forward tracks, and the veto searches only
  upstream of the vertex.

## 6. The objective needs a change (for discussion)

`reward = -[100 (1 - e^-N) + 10 min(signal_lost / 0.10, 1) + cost_term]` (`lib/veto/objective.py`, `score`).

- **The signal term saturates at 10 % loss** (`sig_loss_max = 0.10`): losing 10 % and losing 74 % cost the same
  10 points.
- **Neutrinos make that cap exploitable.** Once neutrinos dominate N, the only way to reduce N is to veto more of
  everything. Cutting nu from 0.6 to 0.06 gains about 40 background points, while the signal can cost at most 10.
  The lexicographic order (background >> signal) was fine for combinatorial background, which can be vetoed for
  free, but not for a background that looks like signal.

Options, best first:
1. A sensitivity figure of merit instead of tiers, e.g. maximise signal efficiency divided by the 90 % CL
   upper limit on N.
2. Leave neutrinos out of N in the optimiser, or count them before the veto.
3. A linear, uncapped signal term with a hard penalty above ~5 % loss.
