[CLAUDE]
Straw tubes appear in two SHiP subsystems: the Spectrometer Straw Tracker
(SST/DSST) and, since 2025, a new straw tracking layer in the Upstream
Background Tagger (DUBT). The 2024 progress report (SPSC-SR-354) never
mentions straws. All the straw detail is in the 2023 ECN3 proposal
(SPSC-P-369) and the 2025 annual report (SPSC-SR-370). None of the three is
a TDR; SR-370 reports progress since the TDR phase began in mid-2024.

1. SPECTROMETER STRAW TRACKER
-----------------------------
Baseline in the 2023 proposal (SPSC-P-369, sec. 3.2.5)

- Role and requirements: measure the trajectories and momenta of charged
  particles, and reconstruct the decay vertex and impact parameter at the
  target. It needs 120 um spatial resolution over a 4 x 6 m2 aperture, with
  low material, and at that time it had to work inside vacuum.
- Layout: four stations, two upstream and two downstream of the magnet.
  Stations on the same side are 2 m apart, with a 5 m gap containing the
  magnet. Each station has four views of two layers each (Y-U-V-Y), with the
  stereo views at about +/-5 deg. That comes to roughly 10,000 channels in
  total.
- Tubes: horizontal tubes, 4 m long and 2 cm in diameter. They follow the
  NA62 design: ultrasonically welded BoPET straws made from 36 um
  Cu/Au-coated Mylar.
- Operation: Ar/CO2 at about 70/30, 1 bar, about 2 kV. The goal is 120 um
  resolution with more than 99% hit efficiency.
- Test-beam result (2017): a 2 m long, 2 cm prototype reached better than
  120 um hit resolution with high efficiency over most of the diameter, even
  with a wire eccentricity up to 2.05 mm.
- Main challenge, sagging: the Mylar cathode relaxes over time but the anode
  wire does not, so sag slowly increases. Three mechanical concepts were
  under study:
    * constant-force springs in an expanding frame (the "piano frame");
    * a PANDA-style "cemented pack" of glued straws;
    * thin carbon cables supporting modules of 64 straws (a 4-straw
      prototype was built in Hamburg in 2019 and has been running for about
      4 years to study relaxation).
- Changes since the 2015 Technical Proposal: the diameter went from 1 to
  2 cm, the pitch was reduced, tubes per layer went from four to two, and
  the detector shrank from a 5 x 10 m2 top-loader to a 4 x 6 m2
  side-loader.
- Production: the technology came from JINR Dubna. A production line was
  planned by FZ Juelich ZEA-1 and Hamburg University.
- Open issues at the time: choosing one mechanical concept, building an
  O(100)-tube prototype module, readout (an FZJ ASIC, VMM3 or TIGER),
  cooling in vacuum, feedthroughs, and the gas system. The proposal rated
  the SST as carrying "relatively large uncertainties".

Updates in the 2025 annual report (SPSC-SR-370, sec. 3.3.4)

- The biggest change: the tracker now sits in air. The decay volume has
  become a helium-filled balloon instead of a vacuum vessel, so the tracker
  no longer needs vacuum. This removes the vacuum-related issues above
  (permeation load, feedthroughs, cooling in vacuum) and simplifies the
  design a lot.
- New positions: the stations are at -5.5, -3.5, +3.5 and +5.5 m from the
  magnet centre, and the two inner stations sit in the magnet pit. The
  Y-U-V-Y geometry with about 5 deg stereo is unchanged. This geometry is
  implemented in FairShip, with the definitions moved to YAML.
- Rates: earlier simulations gave up to about 10 kHz per straw. These need
  to be redone because of layout changes upstream and possible changes to
  the frame material.
- Frames: two parallel I-beam structures about 1 m deep with a 40 cm gap.
  Stainless steel is the baseline, with aluminium and carbon fibre as
  alternatives. Modules go in from the top by crane and are accessed from
  the side.
- Modules: each module has two layers of 16 straws plus a support element,
  either carbon fibres or thin carbon plates. Modules will be assembled
  outside CERN, and prototyping was "about to start".
- Two candidate tube types:
    * NA62-style: welded Mylar, 36 um wall, 50 nm Cu plus 20 nm Au coating.
      Tubes 2 cm x 4 m have been produced at INP Almaty, Kazakhstan, for
      shipment to Hamburg. An Almaty mass-production site was expected to
      be running by the end of 2025.
    * Commercial (Lamina, UK): wound straws with a 30 um wall and 100 nm Al
      on both surfaces. These were ordered by Hamburg for delivery in early
      November 2025.
- Readout candidates:
    * Sampic chip (CEA Saclay/IJCLab): self-triggering, with leading-edge
      digitisation for sub-ns offline timing and a time-over-threshold
      measurement. Test beams with a custom discrete preamp look promising.
    * ATLAS-muon ASD chip (MPI Munich): an upgraded version has a shorter
      peaking time. It was tested within DRD1, including stereo-layer
      tracking, and the analysis is ongoing.
- 2026 test-beam request (sec. 5.4): two weeks at DESY for a full-scale
  prototype, testing mechanics and tracking. Two weeks at the PS to compare
  front-end ASICs (ASD, VMM) against a TimePix telescope, and to show for
  the first time time-referenced tracking using a FASTIC+ timing layer as
  t0.

2. UPSTREAM BACKGROUND TAGGER STRAWS (new in 2025, SR-370 sec. 3.3.2)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
The DUBT baseline is now a layer of scintillating tiles for timing
(~200 ps) followed by a few layers of low-mass straws. The straws track
muons and secondaries from interactions at the end of the muon shield.

- Requirements: more than 99.9% tracking efficiency, about 100 um
  resolution, and ideally fewer than one extra nu/mu interaction in the
  acceptance for 6 x 10^20 PoT. The straws run about 20 mbar above
  atmospheric pressure.
- Rate limits: for comparison, ATLAS TRT 4 mm straws handle about
  0.5 MHz/cm2. Because of the "muon flames" at x ~ +/-2 m, the active area
  is limited to the decay-volume entrance, about 1.3 x 2.7-3.0 m.
- Efficiency: with 5 mm straws, 1 mm dead space and single-straw
  inefficiency delta = 0.5%, six staggered layers give more than 99.99%
  track efficiency.
- Material: the wall is based on the Muon g-2 laminate (Liverpool numbers):
  about 14.6 um of Al/Mylar/epoxy/Mylar/Al/Au. A 25 um W-Re wire and
  Ar:ethane 50:50 gas are listed. That is roughly 0.02% X0 per straw and
  about 1.2% X0 for six layers. Six layers would see about 100 neutrino
  interactions per 10^20 PoT, and simulation of this material's impact is
  ongoing.
- "UBT0" design: tensioned rather than free-standing straws, which helps
  with creep. Wires are tensioned at about 50 g and straws at about 500 g.
  Planes are about 1 x 1 m and build up a 3 x 1 m detector, with two
  horizontal layers and two at +/-15 deg.
    * Each module has two staggered layers of 5 mm straws at 1 mm spacing:
      384 straws, 1152 mm wide.
    * The roughly 2500 N module tension transfers to a frame mounted on
      the muon shield.
    * The open question is how much the frame and manifold material affects
      physics. The fallback is 3 m vertical straws, which would cost
      substantially more.
- People: Liverpool joined in 2025 with straw-tracker expertise aimed at
  the UBT. INP Almaty and Georgia Technical University have straw
  production labs.

I worked from the extracted text, so I haven't inspected the figures
themselves (e.g. Fig. 27 layout, Fig. 22 UBT view, Figs. 39-42 prototypes).
I can look at any of them if you need details from the drawings.

Three things worth knowing for your optimisation:

1. The report itself has a likely factor-of-10 error in the material
   budget. It says 0.02% X0 per straw and 1.2% X0 for six layers. Six times
   0.02% is 0.12%, and its own laminate table adds up to about 0.018% per
   straw. I'd use ~0.12% for six layers and flag it if you cite it.
2. The UBT straws are horizontal, not vertical. The straw ends carry the
   tension, so short vertical straws would put frame material inside the
   acceptance. Vertical orientation is only the fallback, using 3 m long
   straws, and the report says that would "substantially increase the
   expected construction cost". Your table's "max length 1 m" and "UBT
   vertical only" combine two incompatible constraints.
3. A straw's veto time window is set by its maximum drift time, not by
   +/-3 sigma. The documents give no straw time resolution. For the 2 cm
   tubes, Fig. 41 shows drift times reaching hundreds of ns up to roughly
   1 us. For 5 mm straws I'd expect a maximum drift of a few tens of ns (my
   estimate, not from the documents). This is exactly why the UBT pairs the
   straws with a tile timing layer, and why the 2026 test beam aims to use
   a FASTIC+ timing layer as the t0 for the straws.

SCINTILLATING TILES (UBT timing layer, SR-370 sec. 3.3.2)
---------------------------------------------------------
- Size: typically 5 x 5 x 1 cm3, made smaller near the muon flames to keep
  the rate per channel below about 1 MHz.
- Rate: up to 10 MHz per 5 x 5 cm2 tile in the muon flames at x ~ +/-2 m.
- Time resolution: ~200 ps demonstrated with 5 x 5 cm2 tiles.
- Thickness: a trade-off between enough light for timing and as little
  material as possible. My estimate is that 1 cm of plastic scintillator is
  about 2.4% X0, which is about 20 times the six-layer straw tracker.
  Material is the dominant cost of the tiles in your model.
- Two readout options:
    * WLS fibre through a row of tiles, SiPM at each end: fewer SiPMs and
      less material, but you lose the per-tile granularity and the SiPM
      sees the rate summed along the fibre.
    * One SiPM per tile: keeps the pixel granularity, but needs long flex
      cables and many more channels.
- Electronics candidates: PACIFIC++ (from LHCb SciFi Upgrade II), TOFPET2
  (used in SND@LHC), FastIC+ (25 ps internal TDC, 8 channels per ASIC), or
  an FPGA TDC with 100-200 ps bins.
- Channel count for your 1 x 2.7 m plane at 5 x 5 cm2: 20 x 54 = 1,080
  tiles. That is 1,080 channels with one SiPM per tile, or about
  2 x 54 = 108 channels with horizontal WLS readout.

Two related scintillator systems give useful reference points. The
spectrometer timing detector uses EJ200 bars of 144 x 6 x 1 cm: 330 bars,
660 channels, 80-180 ps along a bar (85 ps weighted average), and 30 ps in
2025 laser tests on a single bar. The requirement there is <=100 ps. The
SMTC uses 5 x 5 x 1 cm3 pads, but for calorimetry rather than vetoing.

SCINTILLATING FIBRES
--------------------
- Fibre: Kuraray SCSF-78MJ, 250 um diameter, 2.8 ns decay time.
- Mats: six staggered layers, 1.35 mm thick.
- Readout: Hamamatsu S13552 SiPM arrays at the end of the mat.
- Resolution: ~100 um needed and achieved (as in SND@LHC).
- Planes in the SND: 500 x 500 mm2 in the target tracker (18 planes),
  40-60 cm in the SMTC.

The documents don't give a SciFi time resolution or a maximum length. "No
limit" in your table is optimistic, because light attenuation and the SiPM
at one end limit the practical mat length to a few metres. A 1.35 mm
polystyrene mat is roughly 0.3% X0 (my estimate), which is several times a
straw layer.

SILICON PIXELS
--------------
The documents give no pixel specifications for any veto or tagger. Pixels
appear only as:
- an R&D alternative to emulsion in the SND, in synergy with SND@LHC;
- a movable LHCb-style vertex detector for the proposed TauFV extension;
- the TimePix telescope used as a reference in test beams.

The SHGC prototype uses refurbished CMS silicon strips, not pixels. Your
pixel numbers therefore have to come from outside these documents. As a
sanity check, 55 um pitch is Timepix-class. Timepix4 timing is ~200 ps per
bin, so 50 ps would need LGAD-type or similar sensors with their own cost.
Pixel layers also bring sensor, ASIC and cooling material, typically
several tenths of a percent X0.

probably the better input.

CHECKS ON YOUR TABLE
--------------------
- SciFi, 1 x 2.7 m crossed: 1 m / 250 um = 4,000 channels covers one
  direction only. Crossed layers give 4,000 + 10,800 = 14,800.
- Straws, 1 x 2.7 m: 300 channels means three layers of 100 vertical straws
  at 10 mm pitch. The document design (5 mm horizontal straws, ~6 mm pitch,
  four views of double layers, 1.3 x 3.0 m) gives on the order of
  4,000-4,600 channels.
- The 3 x 6 m fin compares different numbers of layers. Straws give
  3,600 = 4 x (300 + 600), which is four crossed pairs. SciFi gives
  36,000 = 12,000 + 24,000, which is one crossed pair. Pixels give
  6.0 x 10^9, one layer. The fin costs for each technology therefore aren't
  comparable as they stand.
- Straw pitch and resolution: 10 mm pitch with 150 um resolution lies
  between the two document designs. Use 5 mm / ~100 um for the UBT, or
  20 mm / 120 um if you're modelling spectrometer-type tubes.
